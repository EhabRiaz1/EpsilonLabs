Engagements · Plant Visibility Pilot

See every machine that matters, live, in six weeks, without replacing any of them.

A fixed-scope pilot on one line or one site: we read the machines you already have, decoding their protocols where nobody documented them, fit monitoring nodes where there is nothing to read, and put running state, output and alarms on one live dashboard your managers open from anywhere.

Plant Visibility Pilot

Typical length
6 weeks
What you get
6 written deliverables, listed below
What decides the number
How many machines, how many of them speak an undocumented protocol, and whether the site has network coverage. Ten machines on one shed with Wi-Fi sits near the floor; mixed vendors across two buildings needing cellular sits well above it.
Scoping
One call, then a written scope. We say no when it is not ours.

Contact salesGet a quote

01 The terms

Quoted per project, after a call. We do not publish a band for this, because a number without a scope is a guess and you would have to unpick it later anyway.

Tell us what the work has to do and when it has to be done, and you get the scope and the number in writing — or a straight answer that this is not ours.

03 The problem

Breakdowns are found when production stops. Output is counted at the end of the shift. The controllers hold the data, but in a protocol nobody on site can read, and the vendor who could is long gone.

Replacing the machines to get visibility costs more than the problem. Reading them does not.

04 What you get

  • Site survey and a written machine-by-machine plan before any hardware is fitted
  • Protocol decode for the undocumented controllers in scope
  • Epsilon1 monitoring nodes on 5–10 machines, installed and commissioned
  • A live dashboard: running state, output, downtime and alarms, per machine
  • Alerts to the people you name, by WhatsApp or email
  • Handover document and a quote for the rest of the site, priced per machine

05 How it runs

Week 1
Survey, machine list, and the written plan. You approve it before any hardware is ordered.
Weeks 2–4
Decode, fit nodes, commission. Each machine appears on the dashboard as it is done.
Weeks 5–6
Alerts, tuning, training, handover, and the site-wide rollout quote.

06 Whether this is for you

This is for you if

  • Mills, plants, packaging and process lines with equipment from several vendors
  • Operators who learn about downtime from the shift report
  • Sites with controllers nobody can read any more

This is not for you if

  • Safety-instrumented systems or anything that must not be touched while running
  • Sites that want a full SCADA replacement. This is visibility first.
  • Greenfield plants still choosing their automation vendor

07 Proof

eFuelPro reads fuel dispensers whose RS485 protocol was decoded frame by frame with no documentation, and shows every pump on a live ledger the owner reads from anywhere. The pilot applies the same method to machines.

The eFuelPro case study

08 Questions

What happens after the pilot?

You get a per-machine quote for the rest of the site. Nothing is assumed; you decide on the evidence of the pilot.
